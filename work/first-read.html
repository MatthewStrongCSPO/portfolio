Case study · workers’ compensation

First Read

A brief on every new-business submission. The underwriter still decides.

Independent product concept and working prototype. Not affiliated with CopperPoint, and not an engagement, pilot, or shipped company product. Illustrative files only. Nothing writes to a policy system.

Problem

When an agent sends a new workers’ compensation submission, a large share of underwriting time goes to legwork before a decision can start: governing class, payroll mix, three-year losses, experience mod, appetite. Speed of response depends on a mental snapshot in minutes, built from information the agent often did not assemble.

Product

First Read is not a chatbot. It is a sourced brief. The system reads the documents on the file and returns a snapshot of operations, exposure, experience, hazards, appetite, open questions, and a recommended action. Target: on the underwriter’s desk in under 90 seconds. Every line cites a source. Uncertainty stays visible. The model does not quote, bind, or email a producer.

Open the desk prototype. The public build shows an Arizona new-business file with Quote, Refer, and Decline. The spec covers four illustrative files: an in-appetite Phoenix fabricator, a New Mexico framer with an open fall, California roofing out of appetite, and an STP-eligible Mesa HVAC shop.

Guardrails

  • Appetite is the carrier’s, not the model’s.
  • Overrides are captured for guideline review. They are not silent retraining.
  • Human in the loop on every decision that leaves the desk.
  • Not a rating engine. Not a producer tool. Not live accounts.

What I would hold the model to

A demo that looks right is not the bar. The eval set has to say what “good enough” means before launch: cited fields match the source document, uncertainty is labeled when the loss run or class is incomplete, and a recommended decline does not fire on an in-appetite risk. Time to first action is the pilot metric, against a manual first pass. Failures stay visible. They do not get absorbed into a retrain.
